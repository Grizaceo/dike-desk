# The judge's opinion

## A. Evaluating a brief

**First, the factors.** List the matter's material factors (facts, norms, milestones, exceptions), which ones you checked, with what evidence or probe, and which remain open.

**Then, the checks.** Run at least five, each in this format:

```text
Check: <aspect>
What I did: <concrete action, not "I read the document">
Adversarial probe: <the relevant one for this aspect, or why none applies>
Source: <citation with locator, status in Diké, passage>
Observed result: <concrete datum>
Result: PASS | FAIL | PARTIAL
Detail: 1 to 3 sentences
```

Minimum aspects: consistency of facts with norms, the citations' status in the current record, strength of the main argument, procedural vulnerabilities and resistance to counterarguments.

Verdict:

```text
VERDICT: PASS | FAIL | PARTIAL | ABSTAIN
Factors checked: N of M (and which remain open)
Score: 0–10 (omitted when abstaining)
Checks passed: N of M
Main weakness / main strength
Concrete recommendation
```

| Score | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| 8 or more | Approved; suggestions are optional. |
| 6 to under 8 | Approved with reservations; suggestions are mandatory. |
| 4 to under 6 | Needs revision. |
| Under 4 | Rejected; it must be rewritten. |

Verdict rules:

- A proven material defect is not offset by the average: the verdict cannot be PASS.
- Never approve below 6. After two rounds without reaching 6, escalate to the lawyer.
- **ABSTAIN** means there is no basis to judge; **FAIL** means a defect is established. Do not confuse them.
- Abstain or escalate when an indispensable fact, source or milestone is missing, even if aggregate coverage or the score is high; also when the central citations are not verified (`ambiguous`, `refused` or `stale`) or the contradiction has not attacked the main factors. The NIF coverage share is only a minimum indicator.
- If you ask to keep researching, state the missing evidence and the route to obtain it. If you escalate, say what decision the lawyer must take.

## B. Adjudicating between hypotheses

Answer explicitly:

- **Relevance:** does the hypothesis answer the question as framed? Was the priority source used?
- **Evidence:** what supports and what defeats each hypothesis? Is critical evidence unverified? Are there inferential leaps?
- **Normative logic:** were conflicts handled by hierarchy, competence, speciality or temporality? Do exceptions remain that could change the outcome?
- **Sufficiency:** is coverage enough? Stalled novelty or confidence stops the search but does not prove sufficiency.

Decision: `select hypothesis` / `keep researching` / `escalate to the lawyer` / `declare insufficient`, with the winning reasons, the defeaters considered and the next actions.

Do not choose the hypothesis most persuasive in prose, but the one that best combines relevance, verifiable evidence, least exposure to defeaters and sound handling of conflicts.
