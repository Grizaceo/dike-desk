# Foundations of the agentic methodology

LexO's methodology combines three ideas from the state of the art in assisted legal research:

1. **A staged workflow with defined roles:** framing, search, sufficiency critique and synthesis, each with an owner.
2. **Parallel exploration of competing hypotheses**, with an adversarial role that looks for exceptions, forfeitures, limitation periods and nullities.
3. **Epistemic gates:** explicit stopping criteria and escalation to human review on an irresolvable conflict or low confidence. Knowledge is consolidated only with human approval.

## What exists today in Diké Desk

- Deterministic verification of statutory citations by the Diké engine, with a hash-identified corpus and a reference date.
- The brief and its ledger, which are the only accessible persisted state: comments, proposals, decisions and engine notes.
- The NIF as a documentary reasoning artifact. It is handed over explicitly when delegating: there is no NIF board and no automatic update between agents. If it must be kept, it stays only within that brief's scope, never in shared resources or generic memory.
- A team of sub-agents with separate roles (researcher, case designer, drafter, contradictor, judge, procurador). The separation is in the instructions, not in the tools.
- Mandatory human decision: only the lawyer accepts or rejects changes.

## What does not exist yet

There is no formal argumentation engine (for example ASPIC+), no logical solver, no automatic truth maintenance and no statistical weighting of case law. Those are lines of work, not current capabilities: they must not be claimed in an answer.
