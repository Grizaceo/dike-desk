# Sources, hierarchy and interpretation

## Four different questions

Do not merge them into a single scale:

1. **Normative hierarchy:** which norm has higher rank.
2. **Competence:** which body may regulate the matter.
3. **Binding force:** whom the source binds.
4. **Interpretive authority:** how much weight it carries as a reason.

## Norms

- Start with the applicable constitutional norms.
- **Treaties:** identify the specific treaty, its approval, ratification and entry into force. In human rights, consider article 5, second paragraph, of the Constitution. Their rank is debated: do not present it as settled. Review the regime of article 54 No. 1 of the Constitution and do not assume a later statute can amend a treaty.
- **Statute and regulation:** check hierarchy and competence; a regulation cannot contradict the statute it implements.
- Apply **speciality** only after checking hierarchy, competence, temporal validity and subject-matter scope.

## Case law, administrative acts and scholarship

- **Case law:** identify the court, the effect and the scope of the decision. As a general rule, judgments bind only in the cases in which they are delivered (Civil Code, article 3, second paragraph), without prejudice to special regimes.
- **Administrative opinions** (for example, from the Comptroller General or the Labour Directorate): how far they bind the administration depends on the body, its competence and its statute; identify them case by case. They do not bind the courts. An analysis that omits that limit is incomplete.
- **Scholarship:** provides reasons, not binding force.

If a lower source contradicts a higher one, flag the conflict.

## Interpretation

1. First identify the interpretation rules that apply to the type of norm and the subject matter.
2. For statutes, frame the analysis within the Civil Code rules: literal wording and, for an obscure expression, intent or faithful legislative history (article 19); context and correspondence between its parts (article 22); and the subsidiary rule of article 24.
3. Explain how the grammatical, historical, systematic and purposive elements interact. Do not pick them to suit the thesis.
4. If they pull in different directions or a special regime applies, say so and ground the result.

## Bibliography

First, verifiable Chilean sources; then comparable Latin American sources and Chilean institutional documents; global references last. If the exact edition or the metadata are missing, mark it instead of filling it in.
